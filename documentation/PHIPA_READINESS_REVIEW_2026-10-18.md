# Ontario PHIPA readiness review — 18 October 2026

**Decision: real patient data is not approved by this review. Continue with synthetic records until the release gates below have evidence and accountable sign-off.** There are useful implemented safeguards, but material application gaps and unverified operational controls remain. This is a source and local-validation review, not a legal opinion, penetration test, full PIA/TRA, or certification of the hosted service.

## Scope and interpretation

Reviewed application baseline: `e7143a4` (location discovery and private travel enquiries), including the PHP API, database schema/migrations, React portal, marketing sources, tests and deployment documentation. The review branch starts from that active development baseline, which is 24 commits ahead of local main; it does not establish what is deployed. No patient records, private environment files, database dumps, live identity settings or vendor accounts were inspected. No hosted database changes or application changes were made.

Status definitions: **Implemented** means identifiable source behavior, not hosted acceptance; **Partial** means some functionality exists; **Missing** means no complete implementation was found in the reviewed routes/services; **Unverified** means external evidence is required. Missing repository evidence does not prove an organization has no existing policy.

PHIPA applicability depends on custody/control, actual services and agency relationships. Decide whether the clinic, individual practitioners, or both are custodians, and document the platform operator's and vendors' roles. Hosting several clinics does not automatically make a platform a health information network provider (HINP); assess that classification if its services primarily enable two or more custodians to disclose PHI to one another. Regulation 329/04 imposes additional obligations where applicable. [PHIPA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03?search=phipa), [Regulation 329/04](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/040329?search=phipa), [IPC HINP guidance](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2018/12/2018-12-06-ctn-web.pdf).

Names linked to appointments, receipts, clinic attendance and home visits may already be personal health information. Keeping clinical notes out of the pilot does not by itself make real booking data safe to use.

Obligation map for the privacy reviewer: information practices and electronic-service arrangements (s.10), accuracy (s.11), safeguards and breach notification (s.12), retention/transfer/disposal (s.13), contact/public practices (ss.15-16), agents (s.17), consent/capacity/substitute decision-making (ss.18-28), necessary and authorized collection/use/disclosure (ss.29-50), and access/correction (ss.52-55). This is a review map, not an exhaustive statutory interpretation.

## PHIPA obligations versus implementation choices

Accountability, appropriate information practices, reasonable safeguards, necessary collection/use/disclosure, consent where required, access/correction rights and breach duties are legal concerns. Particular controls here (MFA, encrypted backups, audit monitoring, protected export delivery and a PIA/TRA) are recommended means of addressing risk; they are not presented as universally prescribed product features with identical settings. Additional express requirements can depend on provider classification or professional college rules.

The IPC recommends an accountable privacy program with a data inventory, risk review, provider oversight, training, public information practices and ongoing monitoring. It also recognizes implied consent in qualifying care contexts and patient restrictions. [IPC privacy management handbook](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/media/5415/download).

Access and correction requests normally require a response as soon as possible and within 30 days, subject to applicable exceptions/extensions. Intake amendments alone do not discharge those obligations. [IPC access and correction guidance](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/health-organizations/access-and-correction).

Notification duties distinguish affected individuals, reportable IPC circumstances, applicable professional-college reports and annual statistics; do not import a HIPAA-style deadline or use a single harm threshold for every Ontario duty. [IPC breach protocol](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/health-organizations/responding-to-a-privacy-breach/privacy-breach-protocol), [IPC reporting circumstances](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/health-organizations/report-a-privacy-breach), [annual statistics](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/health-organizations/report-a-privacy-breach/annual-reporting-of-privacy-breach-statistics-to-the-commissioner).

This assessment relies on existing safeguards duties and IPC audit guidance, not an assumption that every enacted electronic-audit-log amendment is in force. Confirm commencement/current statutory text with the privacy reviewer before legal sign-off. Ontario e-Laws search results were available, but direct full-text retrieval returned HTTP 403 during this review. Similarly, Canadian hosting alone would not establish compliance; evaluate actual processing locations, access and legal arrangements.

## Required gates before the first real patient record

All gates start **OPEN**. Recommended technical work is subject to implementation and validation; this review does not authorize deployment or certify the platform.

| Gate | Owner | Evidence required to close |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0-1: Scope, custodians and responsibility | Clinic owner + qualified Ontario privacy reviewer | Identify custodians/agents/platform/vendor roles; decide HINP applicability; approve a field/data-flow inventory and permitted pilot scope. Include appointment contacts, addresses, receipts, notification queues, logs, backups and support exports. |
| P0-2: Least-privilege access | Engineering + clinical/privacy owner | Approve a role/field matrix; constrain booking search/address retrieval to actual duties; implement restrictions/revocation that cannot be bypassed through another API. Test unrelated practitioner, reception, accountant, inactive member, other clinic, client A/B, coverage and departure cases. No critical/high unresolved authorization findings. |
| P0-3: Audit completeness and oversight | Engineering + privacy contact + host operator | Cover sensitive list/search/read/export and denial paths; verify attribution/record IDs; exclude secrets/clinical content; restrict log access and alteration, including DB privileges. Demonstrate a review report, anomalous-access response and log backup/retention process. |
| P0-4: Hosting and recovery | Hosting/security owner | Verify deployed version/migrations, HTTPS and private directories, identity/MFA policy, staff session behavior, DB connection security, disk/file/backup encryption and keys, isolation, patching, least-privilege accounts and restore results. Obtain written host evidence; move affected storage/services if the host cannot meet the assessed safeguards. |
| P0-5: Vendor and communications acceptance | Privacy/procurement owner | Review Netfirms, Entra, Graph/mail bridge, VoIP.ms, Google Maps/Places/Routes and any support/monitoring service. Record payloads, locations/subprocessors, permitted use, access, retention/deletion, incident notice and exit terms. Approve actual email/calendar/SMS samples and recipient verification. Disable unaccepted flows. |
| P0-6: Patient rights and retention | Privacy contact + clinical owner + engineering | Publish practices/contact; approve consent/refusal/restriction procedures and profession-specific retention/holds. Demonstrate a synthetic access request, identity/representative check, reviewed export, secure delivery, correction/refusal and complaint process within deadlines. Manual procedures are acceptable only if staffed, controlled and proven for the release scope. |
| P0-7: People and incident readiness | Clinic owner + privacy/security leads | Record training/confidentiality/access commitments, approved device/support practices, incident contacts and notification decisions. Run a lost-account/unauthorized-access tabletop and restore drill; maintain breach and annual-reporting records and succession/transfer arrangements. |
| P0-8: Acceptance and release decision | Engineering + clinic owner + privacy reviewer | Run disposable MySQL integrations and hosted synthetic role/clinic tests, identity expiry/revocation, migration/recovery and messaging acceptance. Obtain a security assessment proportionate to the risk. Retain release revision, configuration/migration evidence, failures and resolutions, approved scope and named sign-off before enabling live collection. |

After these gates, start with the smallest approved dataset and user group, restrict exports and support access, and review audit evidence promptly. Clinical notes, file uploads, dependent access, new integrations or additional custodians need their own impact review before activation.
